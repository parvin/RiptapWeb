Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 9, 2026

Riptap respects your privacy. We aim to collect as little information as possible. This policy explains what Riptap (version 3.2.0 and later) stores on your device, what is shared with the services the app uses, and the choices you have.

Information stored on your device

Riptap stores certain data locally on your device to support gameplay and settings. This includes:

  • Your best score and game statistics, such as games played, taps, play streaks and rank progress.
  • Settings such as music and vibration, and whether you have completed the tutorial.
  • Whether you own Riptap Premium.
  • Your privacy choices for usage analytics and crash reports (see below).
  • Simple game counters used by the app's advertising settings.

This information stays on your device and is not sent to us, except as described below (for example, scores you submit to Game Center). Deleting the app removes most of its locally stored data from your device.

Game Center

If you choose to use Apple Game Center, your scores may be submitted to Game Center leaderboards, where your Game Center name and scores can be seen by other players. Game Center is provided by Apple and is subject to Apple's own terms and privacy practices. See Game Center & Privacy.

Ads

Riptap does not show banner ads or ads that interrupt gameplay. The only ads are optional rewarded video ads: when you miss after scoring at least 10 points, you can choose to watch an ad to continue your run, once per run. You can always choose "End Game" instead. If no ad is available, you can continue for free.

Rewarded ads are provided by Google AdMob. To deliver ads and measure their performance, Google may collect information such as your device type, IP address, approximate location and interactions with ads, as described in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services and the Google Privacy Policy.

Where required by law, for example in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, Google's consent message lets you choose how your data is used for advertising before ads are requested. Your choices are stored on your device. When required, you can change them at any time under Settings › Privacy Choices in the app.

Riptap does not ask for permission to track you across other companies' apps and websites (Apple's App Tracking Transparency), so iOS does not give Riptap or its advertising partners your device's advertising identifier.

Google's advertising consent choices are separate from Riptap's own analytics and crash report settings described below. Agreeing to advertising does not turn on analytics or crash reports.

In-App Purchases

Riptap offers an optional one-time purchase, Riptap Premium. Premium lets you continue a run without watching an ad and gives you one extra life in every run. In-app purchases are processed by Apple through the App Store. We do not collect or store your payment information. You can restore a previous purchase under Settings › Restore Purchases. See the App Store & Privacy information from Apple.

Optional usage analytics (Firebase Analytics)

If you turn on Share usage analytics, Riptap uses Google Firebase Analytics to understand how the game is played so we can improve it. This includes gameplay events such as starting and finishing a game, your score and best score, game length, and whether you open screens such as Settings, the leaderboard or How to Play. It also includes changes to settings such as vibration, taps on the Premium, share and rate buttons, and purchase steps, but not your payment details. Firebase also records basic technical information such as app version, device model, operating system version and approximate country.

Usage analytics is used only to measure and improve Riptap. When it is on, Riptap tells Firebase that advertising storage, advertising use of your data and ad personalisation are not allowed, and Firebase Analytics is configured not to collect your device's vendor identifier (IDFV).

Usage analytics is off by default and is collected only after you explicitly turn it on. Events from before you turn it on are not saved or sent later.

Optional crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics)

If you turn on Share crash reports, Riptap uses Google Firebase Crashlytics to send a report when the app crashes, so we can find and fix technical problems. Crash reports include technical details about the crash, such as the code location, app version, device model, operating system version and a crash reporting installation identifier.

Crash reports have their own setting, separate from usage analytics. Crash reports are off by default and are sent only after you explicitly turn them on. Reports recorded on your device while the setting was off are deleted rather than sent.

Your choices

After you finish the tutorial (and after Google's advertising consent message, if one is shown), Riptap shows a "Help improve Riptap" message where you can turn on usage analytics, crash reports, both or neither. Both start switched off, everywhere in the world. You can change either setting independently at any time under Settings › Privacy.

Saying no, or choosing "Not now", does not affect gameplay, Premium, Game Center or rewarded Continue.

Identifiers and local storage

Riptap starts Firebase only after you turn on usage analytics or crash reports. If both are off, Firebase is not started. Some Google software built into the app may still save small technical status files on your device.

Once Firebase is started, it stores data on your device, including a Firebase installation identifier, an Analytics app instance identifier, and crash report files. Firebase uses the installation identifier to tell app installations apart. Riptap does not link it to your name or contact details.

If you turn on crash reports but not usage analytics, starting Firebase can still create a local Analytics identifier on your device, even though analytics collection stays switched off.

Turning a setting off

  • Turning off Share usage analytics immediately stops future analytics collection and clears the analytics data stored on your device.
  • Turning off Share crash reports immediately stops future crash reporting and deletes crash reports on your device that have not been sent.

Turning a setting off does not automatically erase information that was already sent to Firebase. To ask about information that was already sent, contact us at the address below.

Data retention

Information stored on your device remains until you change a setting, the app clears it, or you delete the app. Information sent to Google is kept according to Google's policies and Firebase settings. According to Firebase's privacy documentation, Crashlytics keeps crash reports and their associated identifiers for 90 days before starting to remove them. Advertising data is handled under the Google Privacy Policy.

Children's Privacy

Riptap is not directed specifically to children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information directly from children. Because ads are provided by Google, Google may collect information as described above when an ad is shown. If you believe a child has provided personal information through Riptap, please contact us and we will look into it.
